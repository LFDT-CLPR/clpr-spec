# CLIP-0002: Permissioned Channels

## 1. Abstract

Today anyone can register a connector on any channel, and nothing gives a channel's creator authority over which connectors serve it. This CLIP lets a channel be created as `OPEN` or `PERMISSIONED`, and introduces a channel admin. An `OPEN` channel behaves exactly as it does today. On a `PERMISSIONED` channel, connector registration on that ledger is restricted to parties the channel admin authorizes. How the channel admin is established and how it authorizes registrants is defined by each ledger specification: the admin may register connectors itself, or maintain an allow list. The access mode is local to each ledger and is never exchanged with the peer.

## 2. Problem

### 2.1 A shared resource with open access

**Current behavior.** A channel has finite capacity that every connector on it shares: the outbound queue (`max_queue_depth`, [Messaging §5](../protocol/messaging.md#5-enqueue)), the per-bundle limits ([Ledger configuration §3](../protocol/ledger-configuration.md#3-throttles)) and the reply queue. Anyone can register a connector on any channel: "Registration is open to any caller" ([Connector setup §2](../protocol/connector-setup.md#2-registration)). The registrant "has no authority over the channel once it exists" ([Channel setup §2](../protocol/channel-setup.md#2-registration)). Only a connector's own admin can remove it ([Connector setup §1 and §5](../protocol/connector-setup.md#1-connector-identity)). Neither the specification nor the Hiero and EVM implementations record who completed a channel.

**Why this is a tragedy of the commons.** The structure matches the classic case:

- **A finite shared resource.** The channel's capacity.
- **Open access, with no way to exclude a user.** Anyone can register, and the channel's creator has no say.
- **Costs that fall on others.** A connector that uses its full queue quota ([Recommendations: channel economics §3](../recommendations/channel-economics.md#3-queue-quotas)) doesn't pay for the slots the other connectors lose. Its stake is refundable, and is at risk only if it fails to pay for execution or sends to a ledger that doesn't know it ([Recommendations: stakes and bonds §2.1](../recommendations/stakes-and-bonds.md#21-when-a-connector-is-slashed)).

The tragedy needs no bad intent. Every connector operator who uses its full quota imposes the cost on the others. The same structure makes deliberate abuse cheap: because each connector has its own queue quota, a party that registers a second connector doubles its share of the queue. The queue monopolization risk is also raised in `swirldslabs/clpr-spec` #75, "Mitigate CLPR Queue monopolization and DOS".

**Why it is inadequate.** On an open channel, no participant can protect its share of the capacity. An operator who needs a fixed set of connectors, for example a ledger that accepts messages only through its own connector, can't get that from the protocol. The only controls are the stake and the application-level checks on each side. Mitigations that allocate or price the shared capacity leave registration open, so each new way an extra connector can draw on the channel needs its own mitigation.

### 2.2 Bad actors

The commons problem needs no bad actor, but open access means the channel cannot tell a bad actor from a heavy honest user, and cannot exclude either. Under the current specification a bad actor can:

- **Take capacity.** Register extra connectors, each with its own queue quota, until the shared queue is full. Slots free only as the peer acknowledges messages.
- **Send toward a ledger that doesn't know its connector.** The destination enqueues a REPLY for every DATA message while processing the bundle ([Messaging §7](../protocol/messaging.md#7-replies)). The queue depth check applies to the application's send, not to REPLY messages ([Messaging §5](../protocol/messaging.md#5-enqueue)), so one party's messages can fill the other ledger's outbound queue. The submitter absorbs the execution cost ([Recommendations: stakes and bonds §2.1](../recommendations/stakes-and-bonds.md#21-when-a-connector-is-slashed)).
- **Address any application on the peer.** CLPR does not restrict targets. It records the connector and sender on each message and passes the channel id, sender and data to the target application, which decides what to accept ([Messaging §5 and §6](../protocol/messaging.md#5-enqueue)).

**Why the stake does not deter this.** A stake is slashed only when a connector fails to pay for execution or when replies report its messages as CONNECTOR_NOT_FOUND. A bad actor who registers on both ledgers and sends only to targets that cost nothing is not slashed. The stake is refundable on removal. On some deployments the minimum stake may be very small.

**The channel's creator has no remedy.** The creator cannot decline a connector, cannot remove one, and cannot tell a bad actor's registration from any other.

## 3. Approaches considered

| Approach | What it does | Why it is not enough alone |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Partition the queue between connectors** (proposed in `swirldslabs/clpr-spec` #75). A portion of the depth is open to all, and the rest is reserved per connector. | A connector can't be locked out of the queue completely. | The reservation shrinks as the connector count grows, and anyone can raise it. It doesn't restrict who registers, and an application that uses every connector still saturates the queue. |
| **B. Separate channels per application or connector.** | Isolates traffic between applications. | Anyone can still register on the new channel. The situation can recur. |
| **C. Stake-to-exposure.** A connector can't authorize more messages than its stake can cover in slashes. | Makes extra messages cost stake. | It limits the economics, not who may register. It needs per-message exposure accounting and larger stakes. |
| **D. Proof of remote registration in the reveal.** The reveal includes a state proof of the peer's connector commitment. | Only connectors confirmed on the peer can send. | It needs cross-ledger proofs of connector state, and it doesn't stop registration on an open peer. |

A, C and D are not rejected. They address different parts of the problem and stay useful on `OPEN` channels. In commons terms, A allocates the shared capacity, B splits the commons, and C prices the use of it. None of them defines who may use the channel, which is the usual remedy when a resource is shared by a bounded group of known parties.

### Requirements

An adequate solution must:

- **R1.** Leave `OPEN` channels, as they are defined today, unchanged and the default.
- **R2.** Let the creator of a channel restrict who may register connectors on it, on each ledger.
- **R3.** Not let a third party change the channel's mode when it is created.
- **R4.** Be platform-neutral: each ledger decides how parties are identified and authorized, including an owner key, an allow list or a governance rule.
- **R5.** Not change any data the ledgers exchange, so a ledger never needs to know the mode of its peer.
- **R6.** Not affect channels that already exist.

## 4. Proposed solution

**Access mode (R1, R5, R6).** Each ledger's side of a channel has an `access_mode`, `OPEN` or `PERMISSIONED`, chosen by the registrant at `completeChannel` and immutable. The default is `OPEN`, which is the current behavior, and channels that exist when a ledger adopts this CLIP are `OPEN`. The mode is local state. It is not part of the shared channel state, the queue metadata or any proof.

**Channel admin and authorized registrants (R2, R4).** On a `PERMISSIONED` channel, the CLPR Service establishes a **channel admin** at `completeChannel`. The channel admin is the authority over connector registration on that ledger, alongside the existing connector admin and the CLPR Service administration. An **authorized registrant** is the channel admin, or any party the ledger's mechanism lets the channel admin authorize. `completeConnector` on a `PERMISSIONED` channel is rejected unless the registrant is an authorized registrant, and a rejected call changes no state. The protocol requires this and what it guarantees. Each ledger specification defines how the channel admin is established and authenticated, who is an authorized registrant, and who becomes the connector admin.

**Signed mode (R3).** The signature in the `completeChannel` reveal MUST cover the access mode when it is `PERMISSIONED`, so a third party who sees the reveal cannot complete the channel with a different mode. Each ledger specification defines its signed message. The message for `OPEN` is unchanged, so existing tooling keeps working.

**Configuration (R1).** Whether a channel is open or permissioned is a configuration of that channel, its `access_mode`. A ledger MAY also fix the mode of new channels or limit the modes allowed, for example permissioned only on a private ledger.

**Two ledgers.** Each ledger enforces its own mode. A connector must still be registered on both ledgers ([Connector setup §3](../protocol/connector-setup.md#3-connectors-on-both-ledgers)), and on a `PERMISSIONED` side an authorized registrant registers it.

| Source side | Destination side | Result |
|---|---|---|
| `OPEN` | `OPEN` | As today. |
| `PERMISSIONED` | `PERMISSIONED` | Connectors can be registered only by each ledger's authorized registrants. |
| `PERMISSIONED` | `OPEN` | Only the source's authorized registrants can register on the source. Anyone can register on the destination, but a connector the source never registered cannot send. |
| `OPEN` | `PERMISSIONED` | Anyone can register and send on the source. The destination answers CONNECTOR_NOT_FOUND for any connector its authorized registrants didn't register, as today. |

A channel that needs protection on both ends SHOULD be `PERMISSIONED` on both ledgers.

## 5. Specification changes

### protocol/channel-setup.md

**Section 2, Registration.** Replace "Registration is open to any caller. The registrant has no authority over the channel once it exists: its key serves only to coordinate registration across the two ledgers, and, on a ledger that derives the channel id from the key (Section 1), to derive the id." with:

> Channel registration is open to any caller. The registrant chooses an access mode for the channel at `completeChannel` (Section 2.3). The registrant's key serves to coordinate registration across the two ledgers and, on a ledger that derives the channel id from the key (Section 1), to derive the id. On a ledger that supports permissioned channels it may also identify the channel admin (Section 2.3).

**Section 2.2, `completeChannel`.** Add an input:

| Input | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `access_mode` | `enum` | `OPEN` or `PERMISSIONED` (Section 2.3). Omitted means `OPEN`. |

Add to the sentence on the signed message: "The signed message MUST cover `access_mode` when it is `PERMISSIONED`." Add a row to the rejection conditions: "`access_mode` is `PERMISSIONED` and this ledger does not support permissioned channels." Change "creates the channel with status ACTIVE, the channel context (Section 3)…" to also include "the access mode (Section 2.3)".

**New Section 2.3, Access mode.**

> A channel's access mode is `OPEN` or `PERMISSIONED`. It is chosen at `completeChannel`, is local to one ledger's side of the channel, and never changes.
>
> - On an `OPEN` channel, connector registration is open to any caller (Connector setup, Section 2). This is the default.
> - On a `PERMISSIONED` channel, connector registration is restricted to authorized registrants.
>
> A **channel admin** is established for a `PERMISSIONED` channel at `completeChannel`. An **authorized registrant** is the channel admin, or a party the ledger's mechanism lets the channel admin authorize, for example through an allow list. The ledger specification MUST define how the channel admin is established and authenticated, who is an authorized registrant, who becomes the connector admin when a registration is authorized, and the status returned when a registration is rejected. Each ledger's Ledger Specifications define them under *Channel admin* (for example Hiero and the EVM family).
>
> The access mode is not part of the shared channel state (Section 6) and is not conveyed to the peer. A ledger MAY fix the access mode of new channels, or limit the modes allowed. A channel that exists when a ledger adopts this module is `OPEN`.

Section 6 (Shared channel state) is unchanged.

### protocol/connector-setup.md

**Section 2, Registration.** Replace "Registration is open to any caller." with:

> On an `OPEN` channel, registration is open to any caller. On a `PERMISSIONED` channel, the reveal is restricted to authorized registrants (Channel setup, Section 2.3).

Add a row to the reveal's rejection conditions: "The channel is `PERMISSIONED` and the registrant is not an authorized registrant." After the table add: "A rejected reveal MUST NOT change state. A ledger that requires the channel id at commit MAY also apply the restriction to `registerConnector`; a pending commitment creates no connector."

### Ledger specifications (ledger-impact)

**ledgers/hiero/ledger.md, Section 5, Registration signatures.**

| Registration | Signed digest |
|---|---|
| Channel, `OPEN` | `keccak256(channel_id)` (unchanged) |
| Channel, `PERMISSIONED` | `keccak256(channel_id ‖ 0x01)` |
| Connector | unchanged |

New subsection, *Channel admin*:

> On a `PERMISSIONED` channel the channel admin is the key `public_key` revealed in `completeChannel`, with its `signature_scheme`. A call is authorized by the channel admin if the transaction is signed by that key. The authorized registrants are the channel admin alone. The `admin` of a connector registered on the channel is the value supplied in the reveal. A reveal by an unauthorized party is rejected with `NOT_CHANNEL_ADMIN`.

**ledgers/hiero/ledger.md, Section 8, CLPR Service operations.** Add to the `proto` block:

```proto
enum ClprChannelAccessMode {
  OPEN = 0;                                     // the default
  PERMISSIONED = 1;
}
```

and add `ClprChannelAccessMode access_mode = 8;` to `ClprCompleteChannelTransactionBody`.

**ledgers/evm/ledger-family.md, Section 5, Registration signatures.**

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Channel message, `OPEN` | `d = keccak256(channelId ‖ serviceAddress)` (unchanged) |
| Channel message, `PERMISSIONED` | `d = keccak256(channelId ‖ serviceAddress ‖ 0x01)` |

New subsection, *Channel admin*:

> On a `PERMISSIONED` channel the channel admin is the address recovered from the signature at `completeChannel`, which equals the last 20 bytes of `keccak256(publicKey)`. A call is authorized by the channel admin if its caller equals that address. The authorized registrants are the channel admin alone. A `completeConnector` by an unauthorized caller reverts with `NotChannelAdmin`.

**ledgers/evm/ledger-family.md, Section 7, CLPR Service interface.** Add a `uint8 accessMode` parameter to `completeChannel` (0 is `OPEN`, 1 is `PERMISSIONED`). The existing function without it remains and is the `OPEN` path.

**Recommendations.** The Recommendations are updated by pull request, not by this CLIP. [CLPR Service](../recommendations/clpr-service.md) adds `access_mode` to the channel record.

## 6. Compatibility and migration

- **Existing channels.** They are `OPEN` and behave exactly as before. There is no migration.
- **Peers.** The access mode is never exchanged, so a ledger that implements this CLIP interoperates with a peer that doesn't. Mixed modes across two ledgers are defined in Section 4.
- **Implementations.** `completeChannel` gains an `access_mode` input. A call that omits it means `OPEN` and uses the unchanged signature. A ledger that does not support permissioned channels accepts the new input, rejects `PERMISSIONED` at `completeChannel`, and needs no other change.
- **Ledger specifications.** Hiero and EVM change as in Section 5. Besu uses the EVM specification. Other ledgers define their own mechanism when they adopt the CLIP.
- **Wire formats.** No change to the endpoint sync schema, the bundle or any proof.

## 7. Security considerations

- **Downgrade.** The mode is immutable and covered by the signature when `PERMISSIONED`, so it cannot be changed after creation or in flight.
- **Credential loss.** How a ledger's mechanism handles a lost or compromised channel admin credential is defined by the ledger specification. A channel can always be closed and a new one created.
- **Scope.** The channel admin controls connector registration only. It does not change existing connectors, the verifier, the trust anchor or the throttles.
- **`OPEN` channels.** They keep every behavior described in Section 2. This CLIP protects only channels that ask for it, and protects them fully only when both ledgers are `PERMISSIONED` (Section 4).

## 8. Implementation advice

Optional.

**Identifiers.** Until this CLIP is Final, implementations use experimental identifiers for the new HAPI field numbers, status codes and contract selectors (CLIP-0001 §6.5, rule 9).

**Hiero consensus node.**

- `ClprChannel` uses fields 1 to 20. Add `ClprChannelAccessMode access_mode = 21` and `proto.Key admin_key = 22`.
- `ClprCompleteChannelTransactionBody` uses fields 1 to 7. Add `ClprChannelAccessMode access_mode = 8`.
- `ClprCompleteChannelHandler` computes the signed digest from the mode and stores the mode and the admin key. `ClprCompleteConnectorHandler` loads the channel and, for a `PERMISSIONED` channel, requires `admin_key` to have signed the transaction, as `ClprCloseChannel` does for the administrator key. Verify that the `ClprChannel` record is not itself a proven structure in bundles.
- The mirror node and the explorer add `access_mode` and the admin key to the importer and to the `/clpr/channels` response.

**EVM (`clpr-smart-contracts`).**

- Append `uint8 accessMode` and `address admin` at the end of the `Channel` struct so existing storage slots don't move.
- `completeChannel` gains an `accessMode` parameter. Keep the existing selector as the `OPEN` path.
- `registerConnector(bytes32)` has no channel id on EVM, so only the reveal can be gated there. Hiero's `registerConnector` carries `channel_id` and requires the channel to exist, so Hiero can also gate the commit.
- The caller check compares the original caller, which the delegate-call routing from `ClprService` preserves. Confirm this.

**Conformance tests.**

1. `OPEN` channel: every existing test passes unchanged.
2. `PERMISSIONED`: an authorized registrant registers and completes a connector, and messages flow in both directions.
3. `PERMISSIONED`, `completeConnector` by an unauthorized party: rejected, and no connector is created.
4. `completeChannel` with `PERMISSIONED` and a signature for the `OPEN` message: rejected. The reverse is also rejected.
5. Replay of an observed reveal with the mode changed: rejected.
6. Channels that exist before the change read as `OPEN`.
7. The four mode combinations in Section 4.
8. Connector operations that stay with the connector admin work on a `PERMISSIONED` channel.
9. Hiero keys of both schemes work, and on EVM the derived address equals the recovered signer.

**Allow list.** The channel admin approves other registrants, for example with `approveRegistrant` and `revokeRegistrant`, and approved parties call `completeConnector` themselves. This CLIP permits it. The reference mechanism in Section 5 doesn't implement it, because it adds state and a revocation story.

## 9. Open issues

1. Should the mode be immutable, as proposed, or should a channel be allowed to move from `PERMISSIONED` to `OPEN` or vice versa?
2. On a ledger that carries the channel id at commit (Hiero), should `registerConnector` also be gated, or only the reveal? On EVM the commit has no channel id, so only the reveal can be gated unless `channel_id` is added to `registerConnector`.
3. On Hiero, should the channel admin be the registration key, as in Section 5, or a separate `admin_key` supplied in `completeChannel` and covered by the signature, as `ClprCompleteConnector` does?
4. Should the Protocol require every ledger to support `PERMISSIONED`, or may a ledger decline?
5. Are `access_mode`, `PERMISSIONED` and `NOT_CHANNEL_ADMIN` the right names?
